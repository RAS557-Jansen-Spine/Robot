Team-01: Analysis of a Bio-Inspired Myriapod Robot with a Modified Hybrid Spine and Jansen Linkage Legs

Jeevan Hebbal Manjunath • Varun Karthik • Yeshwanth Reddy Gurreddy

Arizona State University • October 15, 2025

Initialize System

Abstract

This paper presents the design, kinematic modeling, and dynamic analysis of a centipede-inspired terrestrial locomotion mechanism. Building upon the myriapod microrobot foundation established by Hoffman and Wood, this work investigates the coupled dynamics of an asymmetrically compliant spine and biologically realistic leg kinematics.

We propose a novel hybrid architecture that integrates a rigid anterior spinal connection with a compliant posterior Sarrus linkage, driven by foldable, single-degree-of-freedom Jansen linkages. We derive the kinematic Jacobian and Euler-Lagrange dynamics for a three-segment system to evaluate ground reaction forces, actuator torque requirements, and metabolic cost proxies.

Theoretical results indicate that the proposed morphology may offer a favorable trade-off between straight-line stability and maneuverability compared to fully flexible baselines. A rigorous system identification and experimental validation plan is proposed to verify these findings.

Key Innovations

  • Hybrid Spine Architecture: Integrates a rigid anterior interface for stability with a compliant posterior Sarrus linkage for turning maneuverability.
  • Jansen Linkage Locomotion: Implements a planar, 1-DOF Jansen linkage to generate a biologically relevant ovoid foot locus, improving ground clearance and step repeatability.
  • SCM Fabrication: Utilizes Smart Composite Microstructures (laser-cut cardstock and Kapton flexures) for rapid prototyping of complex articulated structures.

Introduction

Bio-inspired robotics leverages the morphological intelligence of arthropods to solve complex locomotion challenges. Myriapods, specifically centipedes, demonstrate remarkable stability and agility through the coordination of segmented trunks and numerous legs.

Project Rationale

Current designs often utilize simplified rotational leg mechanisms that fail to capture the complex foot trajectories of biological organisms, or rely on uniform spinal compliance which may limit force transmission during rapid acceleration.

Research Question: How does the dynamic coupling between non-uniform spinal flexibility and complex leg kinematics influence the behavior of a multi-legged robotic system?

Impact & Scope

Scope: A three-segment robot with two legs per segment (6 DOF total), fabricated using SCM techniques.

Impact: A robot with a locally rigid anterior spine is expected to exhibit different straight-line stability characteristics compared to a fully flexible counterpart. Simultaneously, the Jansen linkage offers a sophisticated foot trajectory that may enhance ground clearance.

Bio-Inspiration & Scaling

Biological Basis

The design is anchored in the morphology of Lithobius forficatus (stone centipede). These organisms exhibit a segmented trunk where propulsion is generated by metachronal waves of leg movements.

Supporting Literature

  • Bing et al. (2023): Demonstrated that lateral spinal flexion improves motor performance and turning radius in mouse-scale robots.
  • Hoover and Fearing (2008): Established the SCM rapid prototyping process for folded millirobots, which we adopt.
  • Eckert et al. (2015): Highlighted that spine and leg dynamics are tightly coupled; a change in leg trajectory requires spinal tuning.
  • Dürr et al. (2019): Provided the biomimetic foundation for hexapedal locomotion and inter-leg coordination rules.

Mechanism Design

Kinematic Topology

The robot consists of three rigid segments:

  1. Anterior Joint (Seg 1–2): Rigidified to minimize parasitic oscillation during frontal collisions or sensing tasks.
  2. Posterior Joint (Seg 2–3): Utilizes a flexure-based Sarrus linkage, permitting restricted yaw for turning while maintaining rigidity in pitch and roll.

This hybrid approach aims to balance the stability required for sensing with the flexibility needed for maneuverability.

Mathematical Modeling

Kinematics & Jacobian

The foot position \(\mathbf{p}_f(\phi)\) is a nonlinear function of the crank angle \(\phi\). The velocity relationship is:

$$ \dot{\mathbf{p}}_f = J_f(\phi)\dot{\phi} $$

where \( J_f(\phi) = \partial \mathbf{p}_f / \partial \phi \) is the kinematic Jacobian.

The characteristic transmission ratio, \(\ell_J\), derived from leg geometry at mid-stance is:

$$ \ell_J \equiv \|J_f(\phi_\text{ms})\| \approx 0.02 \, \text{m/rad} $$

Force & Power Estimation

To support the robot against gravity with a safety factor of 2, the peak vertical Ground Reaction Force (GRF) is:

$$ F_{z,\text{peak}} \approx \frac{2 \cdot m_\text{robot} g}{N_\text{leg} \beta} \approx 5.5 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{N} $$

Required actuator torque via Jacobian transpose:

$$ \tau_\text{req} \approx \ell_J F_{z,\text{peak}} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{Nm} $$

Required power at 5 Hz (\(\omega \approx 31.4 \, \text{rad/s}\)):

$$ P_\text{req} = \tau_\text{req} \omega \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{W} $$

System Dynamics

The system dynamics are governed by the Euler-Lagrange equation:

$$ M(\mathbf{q})\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + C(\mathbf{q},\dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} + K(\mathbf{q}) = \boldsymbol{\tau} $$

where \(\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^6\) represents the generalized coordinates (3 body yaw angles, 3 leg crank angles). The stiffness matrix \(K(\mathbf{q})\) is modified such that anterior stiffness terms \(k_{\text{ant}} \to \infty\) (rigid connection), while posterior terms retain finite values.

Experimental Validation Plan

To bridge the gap between simulation and physical reality, we propose a rigorous System Identification (SysID) and implementation roadmap.

01. Servo Characterization

We will use a dedicated dynamometer setup with a load cell and optical encoder to generate Torque-Speed curves and estimate internal friction.

02. Stiffness Testing

Flexures and spinal links will be subjected to standard 3-point bending and torsion tests using a precision force gauge to extract \(k_{lin}\) and \(k_{tor}\) values.

03. Friction Coefficient

We will drag the fabricated foot materials across the target substrate at varying normal loads to empirically determine the coefficients of static and kinetic friction (\(\mu_s, \mu_k\)).
